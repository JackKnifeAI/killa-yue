# Keeping: helpers hold an artist's songs while their phone sleeps

Status: built for 1.8.0, 2026-11-05. Code: `HammerKeep`, `HammerKeeper`, `HammerKeeping`, op 32 `KEEP`, app
`Keeping` and the artist page's "While your phone sleeps".

When an artist's phone stops hosting, their songs leave Discover. That happened to an outside artist on
2026-11-04: nobody else carried their songs. Keeping lets the artist ask helpers to hold copies.

## Rules (Alexander, 2026-11-05)

- **Only when the artist asks.** Nothing is copied until the artist turns it on, and turning it off takes every copy
  back.
- **Three copies, not everywhere.** Copying every song to every helper would waste helpers' disks and bandwidth, so
  three helpers hold each artist's songs.
- **Proven helpers.** The three are helpers that have stayed online for long periods.

## The request

`HammerKeep` is a record signed by the artist. It holds:

- the helper it is meant for (that helper's HAMMER pin);
- the artist's public card, where the helper fetches the songs;
- a revision;
- up to 25 song ids;
- a proof-of-work stamp over the artist's id (22 bits, the same as hidden songs), paid once per artist.

Naming the helper means a copy can't be replayed to another helper. A newer revision replaces the older one. Songs
it leaves out are dropped, and an empty list takes everything back. Only the artist's key can ask or take back.

Op 32 answers with the songs of the current request that the helper hosts now, so the phone can show progress. A
refusal comes back with its reason, so the phone moves on to another helper instead of waiting.

## The helper (`HammerKeeper`, desktop helper with `--help-network`)

- Fetches each song from the artist's card the same way a listener downloads it. Each song is checked against its
  hash and the artist's signature, and must allow rehosting.
- Hosts the songs publicly, along with the artist's page, covers, lyrics, albums and station, so Discover shows them
  and the station plays on.
- Works every ten minutes on its own thread, and right after a new request. An artist whose phone is away is tried
  again next round, and a download resumes where it stopped.
- Drops and deletes only songs it fetched for that artist. Anything its owner chose to host stays.
- Bounds:
  - 32 artists;
  - 25 songs and 2 GB per artist;
  - 20 GB in all (`--keep-gb`);
  - the host-wide cap of 256 songs still applies.
- Lets an artist go when their phone hasn't renewed the request in 180 days.
- Owner commands:
  - `kept` lists the artists and their songs;
  - `remove-kept ARTIST_ID` drops an artist and refuses them from then on;
  - `--no-keep` turns keeping off.

## The artist's phone (`HammerKeeping`)

**Proven is this phone's own judgment, never a helper's claim.** At most once an hour, after Discover looks around
or every half hour while hosting, the phone notes which always-on computer helpers (the mix nodes it has met) are
online. For each helper it keeps when it first saw it, how many looks there have been since, and how many found the
helper online.

- **Proven:** first seen at least three days ago, at least six looks, and online at four looks in five or more.
- Past 240 looks the counts are halved, so recent behaviour counts most.
- A helper not seen for a month is forgotten.

When keeping is on, each tend does the following:

1. A changed song list gets a new revision. The list is the artist's public songs that allow rehosting, most played
   first, up to 25.
2. Carriers are filled up to three: proven helpers first, then the steadiest of the others.
3. When a proven helper isn't carrying but an unproven one is, the proven helper is added. Once its copy is whole,
   the unproven one is told to drop its copies.
4. A carrier that hasn't answered for two days is replaced. When it comes back, it's told to drop its copies.
5. A helper that refuses is passed over for a day. That covers:
   - older software, which hangs up three times while online;
   - a full helper;
   - keeping turned off by its owner.
6. Carriers holding everything are reminded twice a day, which renews the request.

Turning keeping off asks every carrier, with a newer revision and an empty list, to delete its copies.

The station's **Ask helpers** button now turns keeping on. Helpers carry the station once they hold its songs, and
the old mixnet path stays only for hidden names.

## The page

Under the station, **While your phone sleeps**:

- **Off:** what it does, said plainly, and **Keep my songs up**.
- **On:**
  - how many helpers hold whole copies, and each helper's progress;
  - whether each helper is proven ("has stayed online for days") or not ("still proving itself");
  - streaming-only songs and songs past 25, which stay on the phone alone;
  - when fewer than three helpers are online, that the other copies go out as more come and stay;
  - **Take them back**, with a confirmation.

While copying, the open page asks again every 45 seconds.

## Honest limits

- Today there are only about two always-on helpers, so an artist gets two copies at most until more people run one.
- Helpers must run 1.8 to keep songs. Older ones are passed over.
- "Different helpers" means different keys, not different operators or networks. One person running three helpers
  could hold all three copies.
- A take-back reaches a helper only when it's online. One that never returns lets the songs go after 180 days.
- A copy is a copy. A helper's owner could keep a file after being told to delete it, as any listener who downloaded
  it could. Only songs the artist marked as allowing rehosting are ever sent.
- H.Q. hosts 245 songs, so under the 256-song cap it has room for only 11 kept songs.

## Tests

`peer/test/HammerIntegration.java`, `keeping`, uses real TLS with an artist host and a helper. It checks that:

- a request meant for another helper is refused, as is one without a stamp (with its reason) and one changed after
  signing;
- both songs are fetched and reported, and stay up while the artist is away;
- a song left out is dropped and its file deleted;
- an older revision changes nothing;
- taking back drops only the kept copies, leaving the owner's own song.

On the artist side, it checks that:

- a helper seen once isn't proven, one online nine looks in ten over three days is, and one online half the time
  isn't;
- nothing is sent before the artist turns keeping on;
- turned on, the songs go to the proven helper, and streaming-only songs are counted apart;
- turned off, every copy is taken back.

194 checks pass on ops.
