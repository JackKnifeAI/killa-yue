# Killa YuE 1.8: a place to hang out

Status: plan, 2026-10-23. Builds on the uncensorable-network design (2026-10-21-uncensorable-network.md), whose
Phase 1 (clean files, saying plainly what is visible) is already done on this branch.

Killa YuE has become the place where people make and share music. 1.8 makes it a place where they **stay**: listen
together, pass songs hand to hand, build on each other's songs, and find each other, without a server, an account
or a company in the middle, and with the artist's safety first.

Rules that hold for everything here:

- No ads, no paid extras, no donation or tip buttons.
- No central server. Our own VPS stays one ordinary volunteer helper, never written into the app.
- We say only what is true: "censorship-resistant", "no off switch", "hides your IP when Protected is on". Never
  "untraceable" or "anonymous".
- Everything is signed by the artist's key, so a song, a page or a station is the same wherever it came from.
- Being seen is a choice. Presence ("I'm online", "I'm in this circle") is opt-in and visible only while the app is
  open.

## The order of work

| # | Milestone | Why this order |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Artist pages and stats | Every other feature links to a person; pages and counts are mostly built already |
| 2 | Stations | The simplest "always something on" reason to open the app |
| 3 | Listening circles | The hangout itself; reuses the station's synced playback |
| 4 | Remix chains | Turns listening into making, and makes songs spread |
| 5 | Nearby: Bluetooth and Wi-Fi Direct sharing | Sharing with no internet at all |
| 6 | The new Discover | Puts all of the above on one screen |
| N | Network track, in parallel: Protected artists over Tor (Arti), the mixnet study | Safety for the people on the network |

## 1. Artist pages

**What a listener sees.** Portrait, name and bio (already in the profile), then:

- **Now**: on air (their station), in a circle (if they chose to show it), or the newest song.
- **Songs and albums**, with covers, timed lyrics and play counts.
- **Remixes**: songs others built from theirs, and the songs theirs grew from.
- **Board**: the artist's posts and the song conversations (Backstage boards already exist).
- **Follow** (the bell), **Share**, **Tune in**.

**How it works without a server.**

- The page is a **signed artist record**: profile, song list, albums, station and links, signed with the artist's key
  and versioned. It is built from what the store already publishes (`publicObjects`, presence, albums).
- Anyone who has the artist's songs keeps the latest record and serves it, the way songs are rehosted today. So the
  page opens even when the artist's phone is off, and nobody can change it without the artist's key.
- The app finds a page by the artist's ID: the phone asks peers it knows, then the DHT, and checks the signature.

**Sharing a page.**

- **In the app:** a link `killayue://a/<artist-id>` and a QR code. Scanning it opens the page from the network.
- **For people without the app:** the sharing phone serves a plain web page of the artist's songs over local Wi-Fi
  or a hotspot, the way 1.2.0's QR sharing already does, with an install link.
- **On the web:** a web link must name some computer. Any helper can serve a read-only artist page at
  `/a/<artist-id>`, and the app offers a link through whichever helper the artist picks (their own computer,
  a friend's). The app never hard-codes one, so no single site going down breaks artist links.

## 1b. Stats that mean something

**What exists today:** hosts count plays, downloads and votes per song and serve them (Discover charts, the Discover
player's counts); the optional community counter (`HammerCensus`) counts installs with tokens unlinked from
identities and keeps no IP or activity log.

**What 1.8 adds:**

- **For artists: "Your week".** Plays, downloads, votes and new followers per song, which songs are rising, remixes
  made from yours, and how many people heard you on your station or in circles. Counted by the hosts that served
  them, as now; summed on the artist's phone. No listener is ever named.
- **For listeners: "Your year in Killa".** Most played songs and artists, hours listened, songs made, styles
  explored. Computed only on the phone, never sent anywhere; shareable as a picture if the listener chooses.
- **Honest counting.** One play counts once per listener key per song per day, so a loop cannot inflate a chart
  (this is the brigading defence in the network design, item 10).

## 2. Stations

A station is a host that plays its songs **in order, for everyone at once**, like radio.

- **Start one** from the Library: pick songs, albums or "everything I share", shuffle or in order.
- **Tune in** from an artist page or Discover's "On air" row. Everyone hears the same song at the same moment.
- **How it works:** the station publishes a signed schedule (song, start time). Listeners fetch songs as they do now
  (rehosted copies help), and play them from the schedule's clock, so a station costs the host little more than
  sharing. Late joiners start mid-song.
- **Shout-outs:** the host can add a short text dedication to the next song ("for everyone in Khartoum tonight").
- **Keeps running offline:** a helper or a friend can carry a station when the artist's phone sleeps, because the
  schedule and songs are signed.

## 3. Listening circles: the hangout

A circle is a room where people listen together and talk. It is the heart of 1.8.

- **Open or invite-only.** Open circles show in Discover. Invite-only circles use the circle keys that already
  exist (`circle.key`): only people with the invite can find or join.
- **Synced playback** from the station clock: everyone hears the same moment.
- **Pass the aux:** a shared queue; anyone can add a song, the room votes what plays next.
- **Reactions** float over the spinning record for everyone, live. **Chat** in a line under the player.
- **Make a song together:** the room writes lyrics and a style together, one member's phone makes the song, and
  everyone watches it being born: the render progress and the time left show to the whole room, and the finished
  song plays for everyone first. Nobody else can do this: it happens on a phone in someone's hand.
- **Size:** messages go through the members' own connections and helpers. A circle is built for tens of people,
  not thousands; large open circles become stations with chat.
- **Safety:** messages are end-to-end encrypted between members (Noise, as all streams are now). In an invite-only
  circle nobody outside can see who is in it; in an open circle members see each other. Moderation follows the
  label-don't-block rule: the circle's creator can remove someone from their circle, nothing more.

## 4. Remix chains

- **Remix** on any shared song opens Create with its style and lyrics (the `.killa` recipe already carries
  everything). The new song records its **parent**: artist ID and song ID, signed.
- **Song family tree:** every song shows what it grew from and what grew from it, across artists. Discover can show
  the biggest trees.
- **Credit travels:** a remix always names the original artist; the original artist sees remixes in "Your week".
- **Answer songs and duets:** "Reply with a song" writes a response verse to someone's lyrics; both show together.
- **Weekly prompt:** one theme a week in Discover (chosen by rotation among volunteer helpers, signed). Everyone's
  songs for it form a shared playlist. No prizes.

## 5. Nearby: share over Bluetooth and Wi-Fi Direct

Sharing that needs no internet, no Wi-Fi network and no cell signal: at a show, in a protest, on a bus, in a country
that has switched the internet off.

- **Find:** while the Nearby screen is open, the phone sends a small Bluetooth LE signal saying "Killa YuE here"
  with a **random ID that changes every few minutes**, so the phone cannot be tracked by it. Nothing is sent while
  Nearby is closed.
- **Choose:** the other person sees "a Killa YuE phone nearby" and the songs or artist page offered, and accepts. No
  pairing, no account.
- **Move the music:**
  - **Wi-Fi Direct** (phone to phone, no router) for songs, albums and the app itself: many times faster than
    Bluetooth (we measure it on the S25 and the S20).
  - **Bluetooth Classic** as the fallback when Wi-Fi Direct cannot connect: slower (a song in tens of seconds), but
    it works on almost every phone.
- **Everything is still verified:** songs and pages carry the artist's signature, so a copy passed hand to hand is
  checked like one from the network, and joins the receiver's rehosted library.
- **Share the app itself** over Nearby, so Killa spreads without an app store or GitHub.
- **Only Android framework APIs**, no Google Play services (no "Nearby Connections"), so it works on de-Googled
  phones.
- Done when: two phones in flight mode with Bluetooth on exchange a song and an artist page, signatures checked,
  in a test we can repeat.

## 6. The new Discover: what will blow people away

1. **Live now**, a strip at the top: stations on air (with the song playing and how many are tuned in), open
   circles (with who's there and the song), and artists online. Tap anything to drop straight in.
2. **The Constellation:** the whole network as a living sky. Songs are stars, sized by plays; remix chains are lines
   between them; circles glow as clusters that pulse with the music playing in them; a new song is born as a
   flash. It is drawn from song relationships, **never from where people are**: no maps, no locations. Pinch to fly
   in, tap a star to hear it.
3. **Fresh off the GPU:** songs made in the last hour, anywhere in the world, with the phone's render time ("made
   in 19 minutes on a phone").
4. **Being born now:** songs being made live in open circles, with their progress, so you can join and hear one
   arrive.
5. **This week's prompt** and its playlist.
6. **Family trees:** the most remixed songs this week, unfolding as you scroll.
7. **Nearby,** when someone close is sharing: "2 Killa phones near you".

## N. The network track: protecting the people on it

### N1. Clean files (done on this branch)

Downloads and streams carry only the tags the artist chose; the artist name is the Killa profile name; already
shared songs were re-cleaned (H.Q. migrated, 245 songs).

### N2. Protected artists over Tor, with Arti (the spike)

As designed in the network plan, item 3: a per-artist switch that publishes and serves only through a Tor onion
service run inside the app by **Arti**, the Tor Project's Rust implementation.

**Honest status:** Arti's own onion-service guide still calls hosting onion services **suitable for testing and
experimentation only**, and warns it may even weaken the privacy of other uses of the same Arti instance; the
`tor-hsservice` crate lists missing features such as denial-of-service resistance. So:

- The spike proves it works on Android (APK size, battery, Opus streaming speed, connecting a listener).
- We **check Arti's changelog for the current status on the day we build** and ship Protected only when the Tor
  Project calls onion-service hosting fit for privacy use, or we can show which missing pieces do not matter for
  us and say so in the app.
- Listeners reaching onion services (client side) is the more mature half and can come first.

### N3. The mixnet study

**Why look at a mixnet.** Tor hides who talks to whom from each relay, but an observer who watches both ends (a
government watching the national internet) can match the timing of traffic going in and out. A **mixnet** defeats
that: every message is wrapped in layers (the Sphinx packet format), sent through mix nodes that each hold messages
for a random delay and shuffle them, and every phone sends steady **cover traffic** so real messages are lost among
fake ones (the Loopix design). Even a global observer cannot match sender to receiver.

**The cost.** Delays (seconds), steady bandwidth for cover traffic (battery and data), and the protection is only
as large as the crowd: a mixnet with a hundred users hides you among a hundred.

**What a Killa mixnet would carry.** Small, sensitive messages, where delay does not matter:

- follows, votes, plays counted, circle joins and chat, DHT lookups for a Protected artist, song requests;
- **not** the audio itself: songs move as now, by rehosting, relays and (for Protected artists) Tor, so the music
  stays fast.

**The options, studied in Rust:**

| Option | What it is | For us |
|---|---|---|
| Nym's network and SDK | Rust, a Loopix-based mixnet with Sphinx and cover traffic | Mature design and code. But its nodes are run for its own token economy, and its SDK's crates.io publication is paused (import from git). Depending on it would put a company's network inside our "no off switch". |
| `sphinx-packet` (Nym's Rust crate, Apache-2.0) and the Loopix papers | The packet format and the mixing design, without Nym's network | The likely path: **our own mix layer on our own helpers**, using a reviewed packet format instead of inventing one |
| Katzenpost | A post-quantum mixnet design | Written in Go; we read it for its post-quantum Sphinx and its design, not as code to embed |

**The plan:**

1. **Study and threat model** (written): what the mixnet must hide, from whom, and at what crowd size it starts to
   help. Answer honestly whether a few hundred helpers and phones are enough.
2. **Spike in Rust, inside `net/`:** Sphinx packets (the existing crate), three hops chosen from volunteer helpers,
   Poisson delays and cover traffic as in Loopix, carried over our libp2p streams. Measure delay, battery and
   data per hour on the S25 and the S20.
3. **Decide** from the numbers: our own mixnet for the small messages above, or Tor alone. We build our own only
   with a reviewed packet format, an outside security review before anyone is told it protects them, and the app
   stating plainly what it hides and what it does not.

### N4. The rest of the network plan

Unchanged from the design doc: Tor with pluggable transports as the last road in, rotating meeting points, peer
exchange, an app that spreads without GitHub (Nearby helps here), Sybil-resistant charts, and a seized phone that
reveals less.

## Not in 1.8

Money of any kind (tips, tickets, paid placement); live audio streaming of a performance (later, through relays);
the JackKnife.io web platform, to be re-evaluated separately.
