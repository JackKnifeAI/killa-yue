# Circles and the board

Status: built for 1.8.0, 2026-11-04. Code: `HammerCircle`, `HammerCircles` (peer), `Circles`, `CircleRoom`,
`CircleActivity` (app), ops 27 to 31. Experimental: not yet reviewed by outside security experts.

A circle is a room where people listen together and talk, with a board that keeps posts for a month. No server
runs it: a few hosts carry it, and every phone in the room works out what's happening from what's been said.

## The record

The person who starts a circle signs a record: an id, a title, a few words, open or invite-only, when it started, a
revision, and up to four public hosts that carry it (their own phone first, then helpers that agreed). The record
pays a proof-of-work stamp (18 bits). Only the starter's key can change it: a host that carries a circle refuses a
newer record signed by anyone else.

The circle's secret is 32 random bytes. The id is a hash of the secret. The record is signed over everything but
the secret, so any host can check it without learning the secret, and the id ties the secret to it.

- **Open circles** put the secret in the record. Hosts list them, so they show in Discover, and anyone can join.
- **Invite-only circles** never give a host the secret. It travels only in the invitation,
  `killayue://c/<id>?k=<secret>&via=<a host>`. Hosts don't list them.

## What's said

Every message is signed by the sender's key for that circle. A phone makes a new key for each circle, so being in
two circles links nothing. The signed message is then sealed with AES-GCM under a key from the circle's secret,
bound to the circle, the message id, its lane and its time. A host keeps the sealed envelope: it can't read it, and
it can't tell who sent it. Each envelope pays a stamp: 16 bits in the room, 18 on the board.

Two lanes:

| Lane | Kinds | Kept by a host |
|---|---|---|
| Room | chat, reactions, here, add a song, vote, now playing | a day, at most 1000, in memory; at most 20 every ten seconds |
| Board | posts and replies, removals | 30 days, at most 500, on disk |

A member posts to every host in the record and reads from the first that answers. Helpers catch up from a circle's
other hosts every minute, so a host that was down fills in. A phone carries only the circles it started.

## Listening together

Anyone in the room can add a song: its signed record, up to three public hosts that have it, and its length. Others
vote. When a song is nearly over, any phone in the room may start the queue's top, after a short random wait, by
saying "now" with a start time. Everyone takes the "now" messages in order of their start, each only once the one
before is nearly over, so if two phones start the next song together, everyone agrees on the earlier one. The
person who started the circle can skip. **Listen along** plays what the room has on, on the same clock as stations,
so everyone hears the same moment.

## Being in a room

- **Reactions** rise over the room for everyone.
- **Who's here:** anyone who said anything in the last five minutes. A phone in the room says "here" every two
  minutes. In an open circle names show; everyone shows by a short tag of their key too, so two people with the
  same name aren't confused.
- **Muting** hides someone on your own phone only.
- **Removing:** the starter can remove someone, which every phone then hides. Nothing more: it can't delete what
  they said from hosts, and someone removed from an invite-only circle still holds its key. The starter should start
  a new circle for that.

## Helpers

A helper carries circles by default, up to 64, and drops the quietest one after 30 days of silence when full.
`--no-circles` turns carrying off. `circles` lists what it carries (sealed) and `forget-circle ID` drops one.

## Honest limits

- A host sees which circle an envelope belongs to, its lane, its size and when it came, and the address of whoever
  connected. Sealing hides the words and who signed them, not that someone took part.
- The room needs at least one of its hosts online. Started from a phone with no helper agreeing, a circle lives
  only while that phone hosts.
- Nobody can stop a member of an invite-only circle from sharing its invitation.
- Making a song together in a circle is not built yet.

## Tests

`peer/test/HammerIntegration.java`: an open circle carried and listed with its key; an impostor's record refused; a
sealed line kept once; a member unsealing it and knowing who said it; the host holding only ciphertext; another
circle's key opening nothing; a moved or changed envelope refused; an invite-only circle carried but not listed, its
key never on the host, opened only with the invitation's key, and a wrong key refused; board posts kept across a
restart; a second host catching up; flooding refused; a queued song carrying its signed record. A probe against a
live helper on ops did the same with two simulated members.
