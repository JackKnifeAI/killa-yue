# How Killa YuE works
Everything in the app, what it does and when to touch it. Open this page again from Settings > How everything works.

# Create
## Song title
The name the song gets in your library.
## Style
The genre, mood, instruments and voice, in plain words ("smooth neo-soul, warm female vocals, 90 bpm"). The chips under it add common styles.
## Lyrics
The words, in sections. The section buttons add [Intro], [Verse], [Pre-Chorus], [Chorus], [Bridge] and [Outro] tags; each section is sung in turn.
## Write with AI
The big gold button under the lyrics. A songwriter model on the phone writes the title, the style and the lyrics from your idea, straight into the song screen. Undo takes them back. The first time, it offers to download its model. It waits while a song is being made: the phone runs one big model at a time.
- Rewrite style and Rewrite lyrics write that part again.
- Sources shows what it looked up, when it is set to look things up first.
- Songwriter model picks or downloads the model it uses.
## Save these lyrics / My saved lyrics
Keeps the lyrics to use again. The name offered is the song's title, or the first sung line when there is no title yet.
## Length
How long the song is. Longer songs take longer to make and need more memory (see Settings > Memory for song length).
## Score
How much of a written score the engine plans before it sings:
- Full score (melody and chords): the most structured songs.
- Melody only: a written melody, the chords left free.
- No score: the engine goes straight to sound. Fastest, least structured.
Show score editor shows the score (ABC notation) so you can read or change it before a song is made.
## Use a recording: cover it or continue it
Pick a recording to cover (sing your lyrics over its music) or continue (carry on from where it ends).
## Generate song / Add to queue
Generate song starts making it now. Add to queue saves it to be made later, one after another; Queue shows what is waiting.

# Advanced settings (Create)
Leave them empty for the defaults; every one is optional.
- Render steps: how many passes turn the plan into sound. Empty means 3, which sounds practically the same as 32 in a fraction of the time; more is slower.
- Variations (1-4): how many takes of the same song to make at once. Each extra take costs time and memory.
- Melody seed: a number that fixes the melody and score. Use the same seed to get the same tune again; "random" picks a new one each time.
- Sound seed: a number that fixes the sound (the singing and the mix) the same way.
- Score creativity: how adventurous the written score is. Lower keeps it safe and simple; higher (the default is 0.7) brings surprises.
- Song creativity: how adventurous the singing and sound are. Lower is steadier; higher (the default is 1.0) is wilder.
- Guidance: how closely the song follows your style and lyrics. Higher follows them more strictly; too high can sound harsh.
- MP3 kbps: the MP3's quality (128 by default). Higher is better sound and a bigger file.
- File format: MP3 (small), WAV 16-bit (CD quality) or WAV 24-bit (studio quality, the biggest).
- Style adapters (LoRA): extra style files that steer the sound, written as file:strength, separated by commas, for example mystyle.safetensors:1.0.

# Library
Every song you have made. Tap a song to play it; the three dots open its options (play, share, lyrics, sheet music, ringtone, lyric video, Open in Studio and more). Albums groups songs; Share the app and Open a recipe are at the top.
## The player
Previous, rewind, play/pause, fast forward and next. Drag the bar to move through the song. The sleep timer stops playback after a while.

# Lyrics
Write and keep lyrics apart from songs. Sync matches the words of a song to the music, so they light up in time and can print under sheet music; Share sends them.

# Settings
## Start engine
Loads the song engine. It starts by itself when you make a song; starting it early saves a wait.
## Skin
The look of the whole app. Install a skin adds one from a file, Remove a skin deletes one you added, Make your own explains how.
## Queue: battery
The queue only starts its next song when the battery is at least this full.
## Wake-up song
An alarm that plays your newest song, the last one the queue made overnight.
## Run on
GPU (Adreno, Vulkan) is fast. CPU is a slow fallback for when the GPU will not work.
## Model quality
Small (2.6 GB), Balanced (2.9 GB) or Best (3.8 GB). Larger is closer to the original model, and slower to load.
## Memory for song length
Short songs, Normal, Long songs or Maximum. Longer songs need this set higher, and higher uses more of the phone's memory.
## Keep everything loaded
Keeps the engine in memory between songs: faster, but it needs much more memory.
## Gentle mode
Songs take longer, and the phone stays smooth while one is made.
## Studio jobs at full speed
Studio jobs such as splitting stems run on the phone's fast cores instead of in the background. Faster, but music may stutter while one runs. Off by default.
## Run the engine in its own app process
Runs the engine apart from the app, so Android's limits on child processes no longer apply. Takes effect with the next song.
## Check for updates
On when the app is installed. Once a day the app asks GitHub for the newest version and tells you when one is out. Update downloads it (it resumes after a dropped connection), checks it against the SHA-256 published with the release, and asks Android to install it; the first time, Android asks you to allow Killa YuE to install apps. Android only accepts an update signed with the same key as your copy, and your songs, projects and models stay. Later asks again tomorrow; Skip this version waits for the next one. Nothing is installed without you, and never while a song is being made. "Check for updates now" asks straight away.
## Allow in background
Lets Android keep the engine running while the app is closed, so songs finish when the screen is off.
## Downloads
Share the models by QR sends the models to another phone on the same Wi-Fi. Album covers, the GPU cover model and the lyric sync model download here; each file is checked against its published checksum.
## Developer options and Device report
Details about the phone and the engine, for reporting problems.

# Studio
A small music studio inside the app. Open it from the Studio tab, or from a song's options with Open in Studio. The round button at the top left goes back.
## The tabs
Vault, Arrange, Keys, AI, Mixer, Stems, Play Along and the Sampler along the bottom; slide the row for the rest. The tab you are on is pressed in and lit.
## Vault
- New from a song: start a project from a song in your library.
- Bring in a file: start a project from any audio file.
- Open a portable project: open a .killa file made on any phone.
- Projects: tap to open. Each one can be renamed, saved as a portable copy, compacted (unused audio removed to save space) or deleted.
## Arrange
The tracks laid out in time.
- ⏮ goes back to the start.
- ▶ plays and ■ stops.
- ↻ turns the loop on and off. To mark a loop, touch and hold the ruler, then drag.
- ↶ undoes the last change and ↷ redoes it. A whole drag counts as one change.
- ☰ shows the history of changes, to go back to any point.
- ⤓ exports the song.
- Tap the timeline to move the playhead; pinch to zoom.
- M mutes a track; S solos it (only soloed tracks are heard).
## Mixer
A gold strip for each track, followed by your groups, the shared Reverb and Delay returns, and Master.
- Swipe left or right through empty strip space. Only a knob or the fader cap takes hold of a drag. On a short screen, swipe up or down through empty space to reach the lower controls.
- The large gold knob pans left or right. Touch the knob and drag up or down; double-tap to centre it. Pan starts in the centre.
- The fader sets volume. Touch its gold cap and drag; double-tap the cap for 0 dB. New groups and returns start at 0 dB. The meter shows the level after the strip's volume and effects.
- M mutes a track, group or return. S solos a track or group. Group solo includes its member tracks; soloing one track still plays it through its group. Group mute silences all its members, including their sends. Mute and solo start off.
- Add group makes a named group, up to eight. Tap a track's name or To line to send it to Master or a group. Tracks start routed straight to Master. Groups feed Master; a group cannot feed another group.
- Tap a group's name to rename or remove it. Removing a group puts its tracks back on Master. Undo restores the group and its routing.
- REV and DLY are sends: they copy some of a track or group's sound into the shared reverb or delay. Drag either small gold knob up or down from 0 to 100; double-tap for 0. Both start at 0 (off). Sends follow that strip's volume, pan and effects. A track's sends are taken before its group's volume and effects.
- Reverb adds a short stereo room tail. Delay repeats at 250 milliseconds, each repeat at 30% of the previous one. The return faders change how much wet sound reaches Master; their M buttons silence it. Returns cannot send into themselves.
- FX rack opens that strip's effects, processed from top to bottom, up to eight slots. New racks are empty. Tap Add effect, then a slot to open Parameters and presets, bypass it, move it earlier or later, or remove it. Bypass leaves the slot in place and fades to dry sound. Parameters and presets are applied together as one undo step when you tap Apply; Cancel discards the form. Presets fill the fields so you can adjust them before applying.
- Trim adjusts level from -24 to +24 dB; it starts at 0 dB. Stereo width starts at 1x (unchanged), 0x makes mono, and 2x widens the difference between left and right. Polarity invert flips both channels; it is active when added and has no amount knob.
- EQ has a low shelf at 120 Hz, an adjustable mid bell, and a high shelf at 8 kHz, each -18 to +18 dB (default 0). Mid frequency starts at 1000 Hz, Q at 1. Low cut (0–2000 Hz) and high cut (0–20000 Hz) start at 0, meaning off. Presets: Flat, Vocal clarity, Warm.
- Compressor reduces peaks above Threshold (-60–0 dB, default -18) by Ratio (1–20:1, default 4). Attack is 0.1–200 ms (10), Release 10–2000 ms (120), Makeup 0–24 dB (0), and Mix 0–1 (1). Both channels share gain reduction to keep the stereo image steady. Presets: Gentle, Vocal, Parallel drums.
- Peak limiter has Ceiling -24–0 dB (default -1), Release 10–2000 ms (80), and Input gain 0–24 dB (0). It catches sample peaks without added delay; it does not measure intersample true peaks. Presets: Safety, Gentle level, More level. The final Master safety limiter remains separate.
- Reverb has Room decay 0.1–0.85 (default 0.65), Damping 0–1 (0.4), Wet mix 0–1 (0.2), and Pre-delay 0–100 ms (12). Presets: Small room, Vocal room, Long space.
- Delay has Time 1–1000 ms (default 250), Feedback 0–0.65 (0.3), Wet mix 0–1 (0.25), and Ping-pong amount 0–1 (0). Ping-pong sends feedback toward the opposite channel. Presets: Slap, Echo, Ping-pong. Changing time glides the delay tap and can bend pitch.
- Saturation adds soft distortion: Drive 0–30 dB (default 6), Tone low-pass 500–20000 Hz (8000), Mix 0–1 (0.5), Output -24–0 dB (-3). Presets: Warm, Crunch, Soft edge.
- Filter mode is 0 low-pass, 1 high-pass or 2 band-pass (default 0). Cutoff is 20–20000 Hz (20000), Resonance Q 0.2–10 (0.7071), and Mix 0–1 (1). Presets: Low-pass, High-pass, Telephone. Frequencies above the project's usable band are limited to 45% of its sample rate.
- Use up to sixteen reverb/delay inserts across the project, including bypassed slots. Shared sends do not use this limit. Each rack still holds up to eight effects. Mix values use 0 for dry and 1 for fully wet.
- Track, group and return effects follow their faders. Master effects run before the Master fader and its safety limiter. The live Chamber pads feed Master; bounce them to a track to use that track's group and sends.
- Every mixer edit is saved and can be undone. One knob or fader drag makes one undo step. Old projects open with empty racks and sends off.
## Automation
Open Automation from Arrange's transport bar or the top of Mixer. Add lane, choose a strip, then Volume, Pan, a send, or an effect parameter. Effect bypass is also available: 0 means enabled, 1 means bypassed. Master has volume and effect lanes; returns have volume, pan and effect lanes.
- New lanes are enabled and start with one point at beat 0 using the control's current value. Empty lanes do nothing. Enabled lanes override their manual control; the mixer faders still show the manual values. Mute / enable returns control to the manual setting without deleting your points.
- Tap the graph to add a point. Drag a point to change its beat and value, snapping to quarter beats. The drag is previewed in the graph and saved once when released, so one Undo reverses the gesture. Cancelling a drag discards its preview.
- Select a point, then Edit point for an exact beat and value, or Delete point to remove it. Beat 0 is the project's beginning. Dropping a point on an existing beat replaces that point; Undo restores both.
- ◀ / ▶ move through the timeline. Zoom in / Zoom out change the visible span from 1 to 256 beats (default 16). The moving line shows playback position. Delete lane removes the lane as one undo step; Undo also works inside the editor.
- Values move between points and hold the first/last value outside them. Filter mode and bypass hold each value until the next point. Points stay at their musical beats when tempo changes. Playback, looping, full-mix export and isolated stems use the lanes; stems omit group, send, return and Master lanes.
- Automation follows an effect when its rack slot moves. Removing an effect, group or track removes its lanes; Undo brings them back. The project supports 128 lanes and 4096 points, including extra points needed at tempo changes. New and older projects start with no automation.
## Stems
Splits a track into four tracks: drums, bass, other and vocals. It runs on the phone, in the background, while you work.
- Download the stems model: about 414 MB, once.
- Split: starts splitting that track. A three-minute song takes roughly 15 to 25 minutes.
- Cancel stops it; Carry on picks up from where it stopped.
- When it is done the four stems join the project as one step and the original is muted, so one Undo takes them away.
## Export
- WAV (float): the full quality the Studio works in.
- WAV 16-bit: CD quality.
- M4A: small, for sharing.
- To the library: the mix becomes a song in your library.
- Stems: every track as its own file, with its track volume, pan and FX rack. Track mute and solo are ignored. Groups, sends and Master effects are left out, so these are separate ingredients for another mix.
- A full mix includes groups, sends and all FX racks. Export allows for insert tails plus four seconds for shared sends. Chains of long delays can extend the tail by several minutes. Stem files stay aligned and include the longest isolated track insert tail. Bypassed inserts add no tail.

# Studio — Play Along
## Record
Open a project, then open Record. Add a track with + Track and tap its round arm indicator. Arming asks for microphone permission the first time. Press Record to play the backing tracks and capture your input; Finish take stops and adds the take to the timeline. A recording uses float WAV at the project's rate. Its waveform is prepared when it finishes. The original audio file remains available through undo.
## Inputs and monitoring
Input offers the default microphone, connected USB audio devices, and Another app's audio. Use headphones when recording a microphone, especially with Input monitoring enabled. Monitoring is off by default. USB devices must be attached before choosing them.
Another app's audio asks Android for screen/audio capture consent. Only apps which allow playback capture can supply sound. A silent source and an app that forbids capture can both produce silence; check the result before keeping a take. Playback capture currently needs manual timing checks and may need a clip nudge.
## Recording compensation
Calibrate measures the current route with audible probes, using a speaker or a physical loopback cable. Use Enter compensation for a known delay or to adjust a Bluetooth route. A saved setting belongs to the connected route and selected input; check it again after changing headphones or interfaces. The recorder also accounts for the Studio limiter's processing delay.
## Tempo, metre and click
New projects use the song score's tempo and metre when available, otherwise 120 BPM and 4/4. Tempo changes the starting tempo; Tap estimates it from successive taps. Metre offers 3/4, 4/4, 6/8 and 7/8. Click toggles the metronome, off by default. Count-in cycles through zero, one and two bars. A count-in clicks before the song starts even when the ordinary metronome is off.
Tempo change at playhead adds a tempo change at the current position. The sequencer follows these changes. Changes can be undone.
## Punch and loop takes
Mark a range by holding and dragging the Arrange ruler. Punch uses that range to keep only the part of a recording inside it; press Punch again to turn it off. Loop uses the Arrange loop region. Recording across loops creates separate takes: the first is audible and subsequent takes start muted. Listen buttons in Record toggle each track's mute state.
## Interrupted takes
The recorder checkpoints the file while recording. Reopening a project recovers an interrupted take and adds it through undo history. If its clock receipt was not saved before interruption, its timing must be checked. A storage or input discontinuity stops the take with an error; recorded audio is retained where possible.
## Markers
Marker at playhead adds a named marker. Jump to marker seeks to one of the saved markers. Markers also appear on the Arrange ruler.

# Studio — Clip editing
## Timeline modes
The extra Arrange transport button cycles Navigate, Move clips and Swipe comp. Navigate keeps scrolling and seeking. Move clips lets you drag a clip to a new position, snapped to a sixteenth-note grid that follows the tempo map. One completed drag is one undo step. Swipe comp copies a selected range from a take into a Comp track, replacing any older comp in that range and muting its source track.
## Clip menu
In Navigate mode, hold a clip to open its menu. Move to playhead uses the beat grid. Nudge accepts positive milliseconds to move later or negative to move earlier. Trim start/end uses the point held inside the clip. Split here makes two clips; Duplicate places a copy after the original. Fade in/out sets both ends in milliseconds. Remove removes the clip from the arrangement, retaining its audio for undo.
## Stretch and pitch
Stretch / pitch renders a new file. Choose 25–400% of the original length, then a pitch shift from −24 to +24 semitones. These controls are independent. The original clip remains in undo history. A long clip can take time; do not start this while recording or generating a song.

# Studio — The Chamber
## Your projects (Vault)
The line above the projects shows how much space they use and how much the phone has free. The ⋯ beside a project opens what you can do with it:
- Open, Rename.
- Wrap up: the finished mix goes into your library as a song, then you can delete the project to give its space back.
- Compact: removes audio files that neither the project nor its undo history uses any more. Nothing you can hear, or get back with Undo, goes.
- Delete: the project and everything in it go; songs in your library stay.
## Put a sound on a pad
1. Tap a pad to choose it. The chosen pad lights up with a ring.
2. Press Sample, then pick a clip from this project, or "A sound file on the phone…" to bring in any audio file.
3. Tap the pad to hear it. Hold it, or press Shape, to tune and shape it.
To play only part of a sound (one bar of a song, one word of a vocal), hold the pad, then set Start and Length in seconds: Start is where in the sound the pad begins, Length how long it plays.
To fill all sixteen pads at once, press Chop and pick one sound: it is cut at its hits and spread across the pads. Kit puts the built-in drums back.
## Pads
Pads opens sixteen playable pads. A fresh bank uses drums synthesized inside the app. Tap a pad to play it and select its pattern row. Multiple pads can be held together. Hold a pad, or press Shape, to edit it. The bank has a 128 MB sample-memory budget; use short slices for large recordings.
## Sample, Chop and Kit
Sample puts a project clip, or a sound file from the phone, on the selected pad. Chop finds transients in a clip and spreads up to sixteen slices across the bank; it divides long sustained gaps when fewer transients are found. Kit restores the synthesized drums. Bank edits are saved with the project and can be undone.
## Perform: Record pads, Clear pad, Bounce to a track
- ● Record pads turns the pattern on and starts playback. Play the pads: each hit lands on the nearest step (swing and tempo changes included), soft or hard by how you touch. Keep it running and play again to add more; one recording pass is one Undo. ■ Stop recording ends it.
- Clear pad empties the chosen pad's steps.
- Bounce to a track renders the pads on their own into a new track called Pads, and switches the pattern off so it is not heard twice. The Pads track is a stem like any other: mix it, mute it, move or edit its clip, or delete it. One Undo brings the pattern back.
## Shape
Playback mode is One-shot by default: a hit plays to the end. Gate plays while held, then releases. Loop repeats while held. Tune shifts playback by −24 to +24 semitones and changes its speed. Reverse plays backwards.
Attack, decay and release are in milliseconds; sustain is a percentage. Defaults are 2 ms attack, 80 ms decay, 100% sustain and 80 ms release. Low-pass defaults to 20000 Hz. Choke group 0 is off; pads in the same nonzero group cut each other off with a short fade.
## Rhythm steps
Select a pad, then tap its numbered steps to cycle off, soft (50%) and hard (100%) velocity. Pattern toggles pattern playback and starts the transport when enabled. 16 / 32 switches pattern length. Swing delays odd sixteenth notes by 0–75% of a step, with 0% as the default. The pattern follows project tempo changes and is included in a mix export. Live taps are auditioned; use the steps to save a beat.

During a take, finish recording before editing the project, changing its loop or tempo, or using undo. Pads remain playable. Other-app capture leaves the other player’s audio focus alone.

Export includes the enabled sampler pattern. A pattern-only project exports one cycle; with audio it repeats across the song. Stem export writes a separate Sampler.wav. The practice click is excluded from exports.

Moving clips into a partial overlap on the same track adds matching fades across the overlap. Undo restores the previous fades. Playback capture stops if the first five seconds stay silent; the source may be paused or may prohibit capture.

Long loop recordings reserve the last of the 64 playback tracks for “More takes (in order)”: later passes are kept as separate clips in sequence on that muted track. Move a wanted pass back to the loop position before comping it. No recorded audio is discarded. Loop recording needs one free track.

## Mastering
Open Mastering at the top of Mixer. Dynamic targets -18 LUFS, Balanced -14 LUFS, and Loud -10 LUFS. Each adds EQ, gain and a -1 dBFS sample-peak limiter after your existing Master effects; leave three slots available. Applying another preset replaces only the mastering effects.
The app renders and measures a separate copy, then applies the result as one undoable change. Cancel leaves the mix unchanged. Before (A) bypasses the three mastering effects; Mastered (B) enables them. Playback and export use the selected side. Remove mastering preserves your other effects.
The dialog shows measured integrated loudness and sample peak. Music with strong peaks may not reach the requested target; the actual measured result is shown. This is not a true-peak meter or limiter. Changes to the mix mark readings stale: apply a preset again to remeasure. Measurement needs at least 0.4 seconds of audible material and temporary free storage.

## Keys: the playable piano
The Keys tab plays and records instrument tracks. At the top: the track and its sound (tap the name to change track or add one; ♪ chooses the sound), ● records, ▶ plays, ⋯ has the rest.
- The piano has all 88 keys. The gold map above it shows the whole keyboard: slide the gold window to move, or use ‹ Oct and Oct ›. − Keys and + Keys show more or fewer keys; Rows gives two rows, the upper one continuing from the lower.
- Where you touch a key sets how hard it plays: near the bottom of the key is loud, near the red felt is soft, like a real key. ⋯ → Keys play at switches to a fixed level.
- Sustain is the pedal: hold it, or double-tap to leave it down, and tap again to lift it. A MIDI keyboard's pedal works too.
- Keys light gold where they are held, by you, a MIDI keyboard or the MIDI effects.
- MIDI keyboards: ⋯ → MIDI keyboards, for USB or Bluetooth MIDI. Velocity and the sustain pedal work; notes outside the window move it to them.
## Recording notes
Tap ● to record what you play (from the screen or a MIDI keyboard) against the song; tap ● or ▶ again to stop. If Count-in is set in Play Along, it counts in from the bars before the playhead. ⋯ → Recording chooses add to the part (overdub) or replace what is there; Snap new recordings tidies the timing to 1/8, 1/16, 1/16 triplets or 1/32. Each take is one Undo step.
## The piano roll
- Draw / move: tap empty space to add a note (you hear it), drag a note to move it. Resize, Velocity and Erase do what they say; in Erase, drag across notes to remove several.
- Two fingers scroll in time and pitch; Zoom shows 4, 8 or 16 beats; Grid sets the snap. Tap the ruler to move the playhead. The view follows the playhead while it plays.
- ⋯ → Quantise moves every note to the grid. Each track holds up to 4096 notes.
## MIDI effects
The gold pedals under the track are its MIDI effects: they change the notes as you play them and as the track plays back, while the notes you wrote stay as they are. Tap a pedal to adjust it (you hear changes at once); hold it to bypass, move, duplicate or remove it. Up to eight in a chain, in order from left to right. Faint gold shadows in the roll show what the effects play.
- Arpeggiator: up, down, up and down, converge, diverge, pinky up, thumb up, as played, random or chords; rate from a bar to 1/32 triplets, one to four octaves, gate, swing, latch (keeps playing after you let go), accents and on-the-grid starts.
- Chord: one finger plays a whole chord: major, minor, sevenths, ninths, sus, sixths, diminished, augmented, power and octaves, or chords that stay in your key; with inversions and open voicings.
- Scale lock: every note lands in the key and scale you pick (major, minor and its modes, pentatonics, blues, Hijaz, Hungarian minor, In, Bhairav and more). Out-of-key notes move to the nearest note, up, down, or are dropped. White keys can play the scale, so any scale is played on the white keys from C4.
- Harmonizer: one or two extra voices a third, fourth, fifth, sixth or octave away, in key.
- Note echo: repeats each note in time, fading, optionally climbing in pitch.
- Strum: rolls chords low to high, high to low, alternating or at random, like a guitar.
- Humanize: small changes to timing, velocity and length, so a part sounds played.
- Velocity: a range, a curve, a fixed level or random.
- Transpose, Key range (drop or fold notes into a range), Note length (fixed, longer or legato), Chance (notes play only sometimes, or every Nth), Note repeat (machine-gun repeat while held, or fixed rolls for trap hats), Random pitch and Swing.
- Mono / legato: one note at a time, for basses and leads.
- Presets: Trance arp, Lo-fi chords, Trap hi-hat rolls, Dub echo, Guitar strum, Never a wrong note, Pop harmony, Played by a human, Rolling bass and Generative sparkle.
- ⋯ → Print effects into the notes writes what the effects play into the track and empties the rack, for editing note by note. Randomness is repeatable: a track sounds the same every time it plays.
## Sounds
♪ opens the sounds.
- Designed on the phone: Killa Pluck, Killa Tines, Killa Keys (an electric piano that barks when played hard), Killa 808, Killa Pad, Killa Bass, Killa Lead and Killa Drums (a kit on the General MIDI drum keys: kick C2, snare D2, clap D♯2, hats F♯2 and A♯2, crash C♯3, toms between). Made once, then ready.
- Recorded instruments: Grand Piano (the Salamander Grand, three velocity layers), Upright Piano, Bright Upright, Organ, String Ensemble, Pizzicato Strings, Harp, Flute, Clarinet, Trumpet, French Horn, Trombone, Marimba, Glockenspiel and Xylophone. Each is a download of 1 to 21 MB, checked before it is used, shared by every project. Hold a recorded sound to remove its download.
- Choosing a sound tunes all 88 keys once (a few seconds for a piano); after that it plays at once.
- Played hard, layered sounds switch to a louder recording, not only a louder level.
## MIDI files
⋯ → Bring in a MIDI file adds each part as an instrument track with the nearest sound (channel 10 becomes Killa Drums). In an empty project you can take the file's tempo. ⋯ → Save as a MIDI file writes the instrument tracks as they are written; the second choice writes them with the effects printed.
## AI: beats and voices
The AI tab makes sound on the phone, one model at a time, never while recording or while a song is being made. Each model downloads the first time. "How to get the best from it" on each card has tips.
- Groove: a drum model trained on 13.6 hours of real drummers plays a groove in one of 18 styles (rock, funk, jazz, latin, hip-hop, soul, pop, afrobeat, reggae, punk, gospel, blues, country, New Orleans, dance, highlife, Middle Eastern, Afro-Cuban), with a drummer's timing and dynamics. Busy makes it play less or more; Feel sets how far the hits sit off the grid, from on the grid to laid back. Choose 2, 4 or 8 bars. It lands at the playhead's bar as notes on a Killa Drums track, so you can edit hits in Keys, add Note repeat or Swing, or give it another sound. Each press plays a new one; undo the ones you do not keep. It runs instantly on the phone, with nothing to download.
- Beat Lab: beats as audio, by Stable Audio Open Small (Powered by Stability AI), about a gigabyte to download once, then a few seconds a beat on the phone. Describe a beat, or tap a genre; your tempo is added for you. Choose 1, 2 or 4 bars; Another take keeps the words and tries a new seed. Restyle turns the selected track into the style you describe. The beat lands on a new track at the playhead, repeated four times.
- Speak: type words, choose one of 54 voices in American or British English, Spanish, French, Hindi, Italian, Japanese, Portuguese or Mandarin, set the speed, and the voice lands on a new track. Punctuation sets the pauses and lift.
- Sing: write words with [Verse] or [Chorus] tags and describe the voice; Sing it opens Create ready to make it. When the song is done, Bring a finished song into this project, then split it in the Splitter and keep the vocals.
## Portable projects
In the Vault, ⋯ → Save a portable copy writes a project as one .killa file: the project, its undo history and every recording, to move to another phone or back it up. Open a portable project opens one; recorded instruments it uses are downloaded again on the new phone. Every file is checked as it comes in.

## The Chamber: repeat, levels and pad songs
Patterns A, B, C and D each hold their own steps, with the same pad sounds, 16/32-step length and swing. A contains your earlier pattern; B/C/D start empty. Tap a letter to edit or play that bank. With Song off, the selected pattern loops. Song order accepts letters separated by spaces (for example A A B A C D), up to 128 entries. Apply enables Song mode; Pattern starts playback. Song mode plays the order once from the project's start. Use the project loop to repeat a song.
Repeat starts off. Tap it to cycle quarter-, eighth- and sixteenth-note repeats, then off. Hold a pad to repeat it; release stops the repeats. Timing follows the tempo map during playback. Repeat also works while stopped.
16 levels starts off. Select a sound first, then enable it: all sixteen pads play that sound, from velocity 1/16 on pad 1 to full velocity on pad 16. This is velocity variation; it does not change pitch. Disable it to choose another pad normally.
Mute pad and Solo pad act on the selected pad, for live hits, patterns and exports. M means muted, S means solo; mute wins. All pads off releases held/repeated sounds even when the input queue is busy.
Record pads captures native-timed touch, repeat and MIDI hits onto the swung sixteenth grid, including their velocities and pattern bank. One recording pass is one Undo step. A capture overflow stops recording with a message. Leaving the Chamber stops pad recording and releases its live inputs.
MIDI connects USB or Bluetooth keyboards: notes 36–51 play pads 1–16 on any channel. Disconnecting releases held pads. Gate and Loop pattern hits are held until the next step; One-shot keeps the sample's natural duration. Song exports and Bounce to a track retain the final pad tail. Every pattern, order and playing-control change supports Undo.

## Killa Gold pad artwork
The Chamber uses gold-framed, dark rubber pads in the Killa Gold skin. A pressed pad glows amber for as long as you hold it; a MIDI note-on flashes it for 120 ms. The selection outline still marks the pad being edited. Pattern playback does not flash this artwork. This is appearance only and has no saved project setting. Other skins keep their existing pads.

## Listen to an unfinished song
Create > Resume a song lists saved checkpoints. Listen to how far it got makes a playable preview from a saved render without changing the checkpoint. It starts only when you choose Listen. The preview may cover the whole song at unfinished quality, or just the chunks begun so far. It can sound rough. Its progress shows elapsed time, without a full-song countdown or render-step bar.
When it plays, Keep in library saves a separate copy; Close leaves the checkpoint available; Resume finishes the original render. Previewing does not teach the normal song-time estimator.

# Discover and Library sharing
HAMMER connects music hosts through authenticated encrypted connections. Discover is the tab between Lyrics and Studio. Hosting and every sharing setting are on the Settings tab, under Sharing and hosting. Library owns your recordings and their sharing settings. Nothing is published just because you import it, download it or choose an image.
## Import and publish a song
Library > Import audio opens Android's file picker. WAV, FLAC and MP3 recordings from other apps and platforms are supported, up to 1 GiB. They are copied into Library and checked by the isolated codec. Imported songs start as Only on this device.
Open a Library song menu > Song sharing. Choose Only on this device, Public network, or Private circle. Add public tags if wanted and choose whether to allow downloading and hosting by others. Apply publishes that recording and its existing cover and starts hosting. Prompts, saved melody tokens, Studio projects and other files are not sent. A downloaded HAMMER track retains its original publisher and download permission when rehosted.
Library shows a sharing label beside each song. Settings > Sharing and hosting > Start hosting starts or stops serving the selected publications. Stopping hosting keeps the music and its sharing settings. Only on this device removes the publication. Downloads other people already made cannot be recalled. Hosting cannot start during generation and does not automatically restart at boot.
The original master is served in place. Compressed Opus streams are generated in memory without a permanent second copy. Editing or moving a published master makes it unavailable until published again.
## Artist profiles
Discover > Your artist page (the card on top) lets you set a public artist name, biography, and genre/search tags, and your portrait. Paint with AI paints a new portrait on this phone from words you give it (your tags to start with), with the same NPU or GPU model as the covers; paint again for another. Upload a picture selects a JPEG, PNG or WebP up to 16 MiB. Use a song cover reuses artwork already made for one of your songs. Painting waits while a song is being made.
Selecting artwork prepares it locally. Publish my page explicitly makes the fields and selected image public and starts hosting. Images are processed in an isolated service, resized to at most 384 pixels per side and re-encoded without original embedded metadata. Withdraw my page removes the profile offered by this host; it does not unpublish its songs or recall copies held elsewhere.
Artist pages list the songs that artist's host serves: their own songs first, then the copies of other artists' songs it carries (Also hosting), with cover art. Your own page lists everything you host, private circle songs included. A signing key identifies the publisher; names are not verified real-world identities. Artist pages and songs are accessible from discovery results. Follow & get new songs remembers the host and enables direct release notifications if Android allows them. A persistent connection notification remains while Live Follow runs and consumes some battery. If notifications are disabled, Android settings opens; enable Live Follow afterwards in Settings > Sharing and hosting > Peers and artists.
## Discovery and search
Public phone hosts advertise through Android network service discovery on the local network. Discover finds these nearby hosts without entering an address or scanning a QR code. Refresh (↻, top right) fetches authenticated song catalogs and signed public artist profiles, and follows bounded batches of signed public directory announcements. Discover shows artists as round portraits and songs as cards with their covers. Typing in the search box filters both as you type; it matches artist names, biographies, tags and song titles locally. Duplicate recording entries are combined for display and their known hosts can provide failover.
This preview is not automatic worldwide search. A fresh installation needs a nearby host or an existing reachable community entry peer. Internet entry deployment, NAT traversal and encrypted relays are still outstanding. It cannot make a carrier-NAT phone reachable by itself. Discovery stops after a bounded batch or time budget and never claims it has searched every host.
Settings > Sharing and hosting has five groups: Hosting (start or stop, your hosted songs), Circles and invitations (public card, private invitation, revoke), Peers and artists (add a peer, followed peers and artists, Live Follow), Downloads (downloaded songs, cancel a transfer) and Computer (the desktop companion). Cards can be pasted or scanned; the QR is an optional representation of an address and pinned identity, not the music itself. Private invitations grant circle access and should be shared privately. Revoke private invitations invalidates them and disconnects current peers. Private invitations never enter public directory announcements.
## Stream, download and host copies
Tap a song card (or its ▶) to listen at the last quality chosen, Auto at first. The ⋯ on a card has Listen · choose the quality: Auto (adaptive Opus), Original (unchanged source), or Data saver (48 kbps Opus). Original is lossless only when the source is lossless. Compressed playback is stereo 48 kHz. A gold-framed player with play/pause, stop and a seek bar sits under the list while a stream is active.
Auto uses 48, 96 or 160 kbps based on transfer time. Playback keeps a bounded 3 MiB cache; hosts cache up to 4 MiB of compressed segments per codec worker. Codec, process and transport overhead is additional. Requests select among authenticated known replicas and fail over when a host leaves; this is not global bandwidth coordination.
Download / resume verifies the complete original audio hash and saves directly into Library. The verified recording has one persistent audio copy. Cover art and public metadata are retained when available. Save to Library in advanced downloaded-song tools remains available for older downloads.
Comments and votes are signed sidecar metadata, not modifications to audio. Public metadata can travel with copies; private-circle annotations stay within their circle. Votes display no account name, but signing keys do not prove unique humans. Hosts can moderate comments on their own copies.
## Release notifications
Follow artists by signing identity. Settings > Sharing and hosting > Peers and artists > Live Follow on / off controls encrypted subscriptions to up to sixteen remembered hosts. Connected publication events trigger notifications without catalog polling. Reconnects catch up with still-available releases and deduplicate previously seen tracks. Old catalogs establish a baseline instead of flooding notifications.
Instant delivery requires live connections. Android sleep restrictions, force-stop, restarts, offline hosts or lost connectivity can delay events. Restarting the phone requires enabling Live Follow again. Turn it off to close subscriptions and release its wake lock.
## Desktop companion and security boundaries
Settings > Sharing and hosting > Computer > Save desktop companion writes hammer-server.zip. The current companion needs a JDK and a terminal; Linux x86-64 has a bundled native library. Windows and macOS remain unvalidated. It can host recordings and signed artist profiles, download and rehost, exchange annotations and watch releases. Its README explains public entry-peer commands. The desktop codec does not yet use Android's isolated service protection.
Only explicitly published objects can be requested over HAMMER. Knowing a file hash does not grant access. Audio, artwork and metadata requests check their owner and visibility. Android media workers receive selected read-only descriptors or bounded packets, not directory access or signing keys. Connection and resource limits reduce abuse; no software is immune to attacks.
HAMMER encrypts transport and pins peer identities. It does not hide IP addresses or traffic timing, encrypt existing master files at rest, prevent compatible third-party clients, or stop recipients recording public streams. Public artist metadata is intended to be discoverable.
The older Nearby browser download action is a separate temporary local HTTP sharing feature. It does not use HAMMER encryption and is not a fallback for encrypted peer connections.

# Backstage and community

## Song conversations
Open a shared song in Discover and choose Song conversation / remixes. Hosts store these signed posts beside the track; posting never changes the music file. Earlier comments remain visible. Choose New post or Reply, enter your display name and message, and optionally a timestamp in seconds. Tap Listen at a timestamp to play that part. Refresh reads the latest posts from this host.
Public conversations can travel with verified downloads. Private conversations are visible only through that host's current circle invitation and are not copied into public track metadata. Hosts can be offline, so a post is only delivered when the host confirms it. There is no offline outgoing message queue in this preview.

## Artist boards
An artist's public profile opens Backstage · artist board. Artists start topics; fans reply. Artists can Pin, Hide or Lock replies on their threads. Muting an author hides them locally and blocks new posts by that identity on your host. Other hosts and people may retain copies of public posts. Each host's thread holds up to 256 posts and moderation actions in this preview.

## Remix links
Publish and host your remix first, then open the original track's public conversation and choose Link one of my remixes. Choose your published track and post a message. The original host receives the remix link; your remix also keeps metadata linking to the original song and its host. Links include signed track information, not private invitations. Opening a link contacts the named host. A link does not guarantee that the host is online or that you have permission to copy or remix the music.

## Listening-circle invitations
Following an artist normally stays local. On the artist's own board, Receive circle invitations separately asks that host to remember your display name and signing identity. This follower list is not public. The artist opens Followers · invite to listening circle and chooses whom to invite. Recipients use Check my invitation and Accept; nothing joins automatically.
An invitation grants access to all tracks in the artist's existing private circle, not a new separate group. Revoke private invitations in Sharing settings rotates the shared circle access and invalidates old invitations, including grants waiting to be collected. Recipients may already have downloaded copies. Stop receiving invitations removes the registration when the artist's host is reachable.
If Live Follow is enabled, the visible connection service also checks artist boards and registered invitation inboxes approximately once per minute. The first board read establishes a baseline; new posts and invitations notify you afterward. Delivery waits for connectivity and for the service to run. Registered followers are limited to 512 per host in this preview.

## Community headcount
Sharing settings → Community headcount settings is off by default. The preview suggests the ops community node; you can save another public counting node while counting is off. Include this installation in the monthly count explicitly enables a check-in when you use the app. Successful check-ins happen once per UTC calendar month; failures back off six hours. Refresh this node's monthly count reads its aggregate total, and includes your installation only if you opted in.
The node receives a separate monthly token and the month. It does not receive your artist identity, hardware identifiers, songs or listening history. The counter keeps tokens only for the current month, to deduplicate repeated check-ins. A network card joins the peer network; peers can see connection addresses, although the counting service does not retain IP logs. Turning the setting off stops future check-ins and attempts to remove the current token; an unavailable node or an in-flight request can leave it counted until next month.
The displayed number is participating installations on this node this month, not verified people or all app users. Reinstalls, additional devices and fabricated tokens can inflate it; users who decline are absent. Do not add totals from separate nodes. All music features work without joining the count.

## Volunteer desktop companion
Save desktop companion in Sharing settings exports a computer package. The preview adds optional peer networking, artist boards, invitations and the monthly counter. Its README explains the daemon flags and limits. The ops volunteer node hosts metadata and performs audio transcoding for hosted tracks. Remote music generation is not available in this milestone. The companion roadmap covers a full Rust desktop music app, tested GPU support and future volunteer generation workers.
